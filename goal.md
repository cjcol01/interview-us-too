# Goal: Viral Marketing Stunt Concepts

## Objective

Generate marketing stunt concepts with genuine viral potential for a small UK business. Optimise for involuntary sharing, not for polish.

## Business context

- **Business:** InterviewWise
- **What it sells:** Chrome extension giving real-time AI assistance during job interviews. Subscription SaaS, Stripe billing.
- **Audience:** Job seekers, English-speaking, global. Skews early-career and tech. Reached largely through paid social, so assets must survive Meta and TikTok ad review.
- **Named competitors:** Cluely (primary), Final Round AI, Interview Coder. Cluely is itself a stunt-marketing company, which means out-executing them on spectacle is contested ground but beating them on verifiable substance is open ground. Their documented security incident is a matter of public record and is fair comparative material if stated accurately.
- **Budget ceiling:** TBC
- **Appetite for destroying real value (£):** TBC
- **The true sector outrage:** Employers already screen candidates with automated systems at scale. Applicants are filtered, ranked and rejected by machines, often without a human reading the application, and are then told to perform sincerity in one-way recorded video interviews. This is true, documented, and most candidates have felt it without having it named. It is the strongest outrage vein available and it is asymmetric: it makes the product's existence an argument rather than a confession.

### Product-specific hazards

- **The category is contested.** Concepts that frame the product as cheating enablement will generate outrage pointed at the business rather than at the sector. Same volume, inverted sign. Every concept must state which direction the outrage travels.
- **No physical inventory.** The costly-signal trigger cannot be satisfied by burning stock. It has to come from destroying something else real: revenue, a paid asset, an acquisition channel, or a publicly refunded cohort. Anything claimed here must be provable from Stripe records.
- **Pre-launch timing.** Concepts should work with a small or zero existing user base. Anything requiring an established community is out of scope for now.

## The mechanism to target

Content spreads involuntarily when it triggers one of four reactions. Every concept must name which one it is aiming at, explicitly.

1. **Disbelief.** "There is no way that is real." Requires something genuinely improbable that survives scrutiny.
2. **Outrage.** A true thing the audience did not know and is angry about. Outrage outperforms sympathy by a wide margin.
3. **Transgression.** Something that feels like it should not be allowed but is. The gap between "surely illegal" and "actually lawful" is where this lives.
4. **Costly signal.** Visible, irreversible destruction of real value. The KLF burning a million pounds. Banksy shredding the painting at auction.

A concept hitting two of these beats one hitting a single trigger harder.

## Reference concept: the register to hit, and the execution that is out of bounds

The following was considered and rejected. It is included because it correctly identifies the emotional register the output should reach. The register is the target. The execution is prohibited.

**The rejected concept:** a staged campaign of escalating aggression from an unnamed rival. A fabricated threatening letter, then CCTV of the owner being assaulted, then a faked autopsy report showing the owner's dog had been poisoned, then a wall on the property set alight. Presented as real, with a disclaimer added for cover.

**Why the register is right.** Four beats of escalating jeopardy, each raising the stakes, with an antagonist applying pressure and a protagonist refusing to fold. Serial rather than single, so it generates return visits rather than one spike. It is genuinely uncomfortable and it makes the audience take a side. Output should aim at this level of intensity and this shape.

**Why the execution is prohibited.** Presenting a fabricated attack as real, in order to drive attention and sales, is dishonestly making a false representation for gain under the Fraud Act 2006 s.2. Publishing staged assault footage predictably triggers a police response, which brings wasting police time and, because the evidence was manufactured, perverting the course of justice. The autopsy engages Online Safety Act 2023 s.179 on knowingly false communications intended to cause non-trivial psychological harm. An unnamed rival does not solve the civil exposure, because reference to a small class identifies every member of it, giving each of them a claim in defamation and in malicious falsehood. A disclaimer does not rescue it: to negate the representation it has to reach the same audience with the same force as the claim, which is the exact point at which the concept stops working. And the clip travels without the caption anyway.

**The generalisable rule.** The fabrication is what fails, not the intensity. Do not soften the register in response to this section. Find real jeopardy, real antagonists, or real destruction that reaches the same emotional pitch. Note that the strongest available antagonist for this business is not invented and not a competitor: it is the automated hiring apparatus itself, which is real, documented, genuinely adversarial to the audience, and cannot sue.

## Hard constraints

These are not stylistic preferences. Each one killed a previously considered concept.

- **No fabricated victimhood.** No staged crime against the business or owner, no invented antagonist, no fake threats, assaults, or harm to animals. Presenting a fabricated attack as real is a false representation for gain under the Fraud Act 2006 s.2. A disclaimer does not fix it, because the disclaimer's effectiveness and the stunt's effect are the same dial: turn it far enough to be safe and the stunt stops working.
- **No unverifiable claims.** If the concept depends on the audience believing something, that something must be checkable and true. Anything that cannot survive a journalist verifying it is out, because the uncovering becomes the story and replaces the original reach.
- **Named competitors are permitted if the comparison is accurate.** Comparative advertising is lawful under the Business Protection from Misleading Marketing Regulations 2008 where the comparison is like for like, factually correct, and does not denigrate. This is the cheapest available source of real transgression and almost nobody uses it.
- **Truth is an absolute defence to defamation.** Naming and evidencing something true about a competitor or the sector is a stronger position than insinuating about an unnamed one, not weaker.
- **Fire and pyrotechnics require a licensed SFX supervisor.** They handle the DSEAR risk assessment and notify the local fire and rescue service. No accelerant applied to a surface then ignited from a distance: the injury mechanism is vapour flash, not flame. Gas bar or controlled flame effect instead.
- **ASA CAP Code applies.** Rule 4.2 (fear or distress without justification), 4.5 (condoning unsafe practice or violence), 1.3 (social responsibility).
- **Platform policy is a separate gate from legality.** Meta, TikTok and YouTube prohibit depictions of incendiary weapons and graphic animal content in ads. A concept can be perfectly lawful and still leave the asset unusable, meaning all the risk was absorbed for nothing. Check this before, not after.
- **Insurance.** Any deliberate destruction voids standard cover unless the insurer is told in advance. Flag this on any concept involving fire or property damage.

## Craft notes

- **Production value going down is what reads as real.** One phone, one continuous take, no grade, no music, no logo. The moment it looks made, it reads as a marketing stunt and the sharing impulse dies. Half the reason crime footage performs is the format, not the content, and that format is available honestly.
- **Serial beats single.** An escalation ladder over weeks generates return visits. A single event generates one spike. Prefer structures with four or more beats.
- **Real jeopardy is detectable and so is its absence.** If the concept involves a target or a forfeit, the target must be one that might genuinely be missed. A number the business would hit comfortably reads as theatre within one post.
- **Optimise for intent, not views.** Horror-driven virality produces the highest-volume, lowest-intent traffic there is: people watch, wince, share, and never think about the business again. A tenth of the views from people invested in an outcome converts better. State the expected view-to-customer profile for each concept rather than just projected reach.
- **Give press a reason to cover rather than aggregate.** A true argument attached to the spectacle is what turns a clip into an article.

## Output format

Produce 5 to 7 concepts. For each:

- **One-line pitch.**
- **Trigger(s)** from the four above, named explicitly.
- **The first three seconds.** What is literally on screen. If this does not stop a scroll, the rest does not matter.
- **Escalation structure**, if serial. Beat by beat.
- **What is real and verifiable about it.** Be specific about what a journalist could check.
- **Legal and regulatory check.** Which of the hard constraints it touches and how it clears them. Flag anything needing a media lawyer or SFX supervisor.
- **Platform check.** Will this survive as a paid asset on Meta and TikTok.
- **Cost.**
- **Failure mode.** What it looks like if it lands badly, and whether that is recoverable.

Then rank them: highest ceiling first, then most robust, then cheapest to test.

## Anti-goal

Do not produce a list of safe, forgettable ideas. The constraints above rule out one specific category (fabricated victimhood), not risk in general. Concepts should still be genuinely uncomfortable to green-light. If every concept feels safe to sign off, the brief has been read too conservatively and the output is worthless.
